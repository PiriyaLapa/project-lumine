## Lessons Learned

- **After any session resume, gap, or failed `/compact`, verify current git/deployment/issue-tracker state before writing a retrospective, a handoff, or taking any action that assumes continuity** — a session ID staying the same does not mean the world hasn't moved. The cost of checking is a few read-only commands; the cost of not checking is producing a confidently wrong retro and a handoff that misdirects the next session toward stale reality.
- **When you discover your own prior diagnosis was superseded by later, better evidence, don't quietly let the retro imply it was still correct** — name the supersession explicitly (what you thought vs. what was actually found), even when the earlier work wasn't wrong given what was known at the time. This is different from claiming the earlier fix was worthless — the race-condition fix was real and probably still worth having; it just wasn't the whole story.
- **A large, silent gap between two turns of the "same" session is a legitimate trigger for treating the moment like a fresh session start** (i.e., closer to `/recap` than to blind continuation) — even when the conversation's own session ID and immediate prior message suggest otherwise.
